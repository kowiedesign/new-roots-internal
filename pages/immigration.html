Business Manager Visa Reform

Effective October 16, 2025, Japan substantially tightened the Business Manager (経営・管理) visa. This is the category the current owner holds.

Before Oct 2025Since Oct 16, 2025
Minimum capital¥5M¥30M
Test structureCapital or staffingCapital and staffing and Japanese language (~N2) and 3+ years management experience or a relevant master's
Staffing detail2+ full-time employees, any status1+ full-time employee holding Japanese nationality, special permanent residence, or an Appended Table 2 status (Permanent Resident, Spouse/Child of Japanese National, Spouse/Child of Permanent Resident, Long-Term Resident) — most Table 1 work-visa holders don't count
Existing holders—Discretionary transition window for renewals through Oct 16, 2028. Case-by-case, based on business performance and progress toward the new standard — not automatic.
Related restriction—If revised criteria aren't met: no change of status from Business Manager to HSP (i), and no grant of Permanent Residence while on Business Manager status

New applications fell from roughly 1,700/month to about 70/month immediately after the reform, per the Immigration Services Agency.

The Owner's Expected Path

Rather than staying on a Business Manager visa, the plan is for him to transition to an employment-based status — likely Skilled Labor (chef-specific, if his background qualifies) or another Engineer/Specialist-type category, as a New Roots Collective employee.

Working assumption, not confirmed: under this plan, New Roots Collective would not need to meet the ¥30M Business Manager capitalization threshold, since that requirement is specific to the Business Manager category, not to sponsoring an employee's visa. Changing status of residence has its own requirements, and a chef-specific visa has its own duties/skill-matching criteria — both need professional verification.

What this could mean for us

  • Corey: HSP status typically carries an accelerated PR track (as little as 1–3 years under the points system). This isn't reported as a direct target of the Oct 2026 changes, but the income and pension thresholds would still apply whenever he applies. Since he isn't planning to apply in the near term, the main effect is on when to apply, not on this acquisition. Confirm with a specialist whether HSP's accelerated track is exempt from any new criteria.
  • May: if she pursues PR later, the same fee, income, and pension thresholds would apply. Her Child of Japanese National status may still offer a shorter qualifying period than a standard work-visa route — confirm against the new guidelines.
  • The owner: the restriction on granting PR to Business Manager holders who don't meet the new criteria is one more reason the planned move to an employment-based visa (above) makes sense, independent of the capital question.
  • Timing detail: the fee jump only matters if a PR application is filed before vs. after Oct 1, 2026. Not currently relevant since no one is applying now, but worth knowing the cutoff exists.
  • Naturalization change: a different track from PR. Only relevant if either of us considers Japanese citizenship, which hasn't been discussed.
