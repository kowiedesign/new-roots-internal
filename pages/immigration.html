1. Acquisition Decision

What immigration considerations affect this acquisition, and what should we do? Two groups matter: the current owner and restaurant staff, and Corey and May. General visa/PR background is in Section 2 — this section references it rather than repeating it.

1.1 Current Owner and Restaurant Staff

Current situation

  • Owner holds a Business Manager (経営・管理) visa — the category tightened Oct 16, 2025 (see reform detail).
  • Deal premise: owner stays on as operator, moving from owner to employee. Decided — see Decision Document.
  • Restaurant staff: headcount, roles, tenure, and visa categories are all currently unknown.

Assumptions (unconfirmed)

  • Owner transitions to an employment-based visa — Skilled Labor (chef-specific) or another Engineer/Specialist category — as an NRC employee, rather than renewing Business Manager.
  • Under that path, NRC would not need to meet the ¥30M Business Manager capitalization threshold, since that requirement attaches to the Business Manager category, not to sponsoring an employee's visa.
  • No current staff member's status depends on NRC meeting Business Manager staffing tests, consistent with the owner leaving that category — holds only if the assumption above holds.
Upsides
  • A stable visa outcome for the owner is a genuine good on its own, independent of the acquisition's financial return.
  • Avoiding the Business Manager category removes the ¥30M capital requirement and its language/experience tests.
Genuine risks
  • A chef/skilled-labor visa may not be available to the owner if his background doesn't match the visa's skill-matching criteria.
  • If the assumed visa path falls through, NRC may need to reconsider capitalization or acquisition structure entirely.
Unknowns
  • The owner's exact Business Manager renewal date within the 2025–2028 discretionary window.
  • Whether a chef/skilled-labor visa is realistically available to him.
  • Each staff member's current visa category.

Dependencies

  • Written confirmation from a licensed immigration specialist (targeted: August 6 meeting).
  • Staff visa data collection — not yet started.
Recommendation: Proceed on the assumption that the owner moves to an employment-based visa as an NRC employee; do not plan around the ¥30M Business Manager threshold. Do not finalize acquisition structure until a specialist confirms this path in writing.

Decision status: Recommended — pending professional confirmation.

2. General Immigration Reference

Background only. Someone already familiar with Japanese immigration rules can skip this section without missing any project-specific decision — those are all in Section 1.

Business Manager Visa Reform

Effective October 16, 2025, Japan substantially tightened the Business Manager (経営・管理) visa.

Before Oct 2025Since Oct 16, 2025
Minimum capital¥5M¥30M
Test structureCapital or staffingCapital and staffing and Japanese language (~N2) and 3+ years management experience or a relevant master's
Staffing detail2+ full-time employees, any status1+ full-time employee holding Japanese nationality, special permanent residence, or an Appended Table 2 status (Permanent Resident, Spouse/Child of Japanese National, Spouse/Child of Permanent Resident, Long-Term Resident) — most Table 1 work-visa holders don't count
Existing holders—Discretionary transition window for renewals through Oct 16, 2028. Case-by-case, based on business performance and progress toward the new standard — not automatic.
Related restriction—If revised criteria aren't met: no change of status from Business Manager to HSP (i), and no grant of Permanent Residence while on Business Manager status

New applications fell from roughly 1,700/month to about 70/month immediately after the reform, per the Immigration Services Agency.
